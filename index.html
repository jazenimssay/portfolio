Chapter I

The Origin Story

I’ve always been the kind of person who looks at something and thinks, I could probably make this myself.

I started with graphic design, fascinated by visuals and the craft of making things. Then came a deeper curiosity about the people on the other side of the screen. Why do we behave the way we do? What makes an experience feel intuitive? What makes people connect with something?

That curiosity led me from psychology and human nature into UX research and product design, where understanding people became part of how I work.

At some point I just needed a place to put all of it. A portfolio to display the work, the experience, and all the hours that went into getting here.

So began the quest for the perfect portfolio.

First came Behance, which just didn’t do it for me. Then Notion, which, kudos, was almost there. Then I fell further down the rabbit hole into Framer, and alas, Webflow.

At that point I figured I might as well learn some front-end and build the thing from scratch. Two birds, one stone: I could make my portfolio exactly how I imagined it, while learning enough to understand developers better and make handoffs a little easier.

So I learned HTML and CSS, and then realised backend was probably next, but decided vibe coding could take care of that part for now. That’s a quest for another day.

And here we are. Today I’m somewhere between designer, researcher, problem-solver and explorer. I enjoy turning complicated concepts into something simple that anyone can understand, and occasionally going far deeper down a rabbit hole than necessary.

Curiosity, craft, and a slightly unreasonable number of side quests. That’s pretty much my origin story.

Chapter V

Notes in the Margins

Don’t ask me about the reading list.

The things that aren’t on the CV but show up in my work anyway.

I ask why a lot

Not in the annoying way. I just have a hard time accepting “that’s how it’s done” as an answer. I like getting to the reason behind a decision, whether it’s a user behaviour, a design choice or a business constraint. Though I tend to be self-reserved, it has yet to stop me from asking.

Don’t worry, I do ask for help when I know it would save unnecessary time and energy.
I’m weirdly self-sufficient

Give me a problem and I’ll usually try to solve it before asking for help. Sometimes that means figuring out a new tool, digging through documentation, or going down an unnecessarily deep Google rabbit hole. It also means I’m comfortable being thrown into unfamiliar territory and finding my way around.

I’m good at connecting dots

I tend to pull ideas from completely different places and see what happens when they meet. Psychology influences how I think about UX. Craft influences how I approach visual work. Branding influences how I think about products. Random things I learn for fun somehow end up being useful six months later.
